## Context

PLAN.md **§2 / D15** lists delayed delivery first among the phase-2 features and says why: it is
*"nearly free on the `visible_at` machinery"*. This issue is where we find out whether that is true.
The answer is *mostly* — the scheduling itself is one nullable column and one `COALESCE` in the
top-up insert — and the honest remainder is the two things a naive implementation gets wrong:
**flow control** (a scheduled backlog must not eat the `max_ack_pending` budget and starve live
traffic) and **accounting** (a message intentionally parked for six hours must not read as six hours
of lag on `messq_oldest_pending_age_seconds`, the SLI PLAN §9.4 tells operators to alert on).

The feature: a publisher sets `Messq-Deliver-At` (absolute) or `Messq-Delay` (relative); the message
exists in the stream, is inspectable and traceable — and is delivered to **no** consumer until that
instant. Delayed retries, "remind me in 24 h", "publish now, release at 09:00", back-off
orchestration in the broker instead of a `sleep` inside a worker.

Governing material:

- **§2 / D5 — delivery state is a row with a `visible_at` timestamp; timeouts are one indexed
  `UPDATE` in a sweeper tick, not per-message timers.** Delayed delivery is the same mechanism with
  the timestamp set at admission instead of at nak. No new scheduler, no timing wheel, no new
  goroutine — the §3.2 goroutine census is unchanged by this issue, and that is the single most
  important sentence in it.
- **§5 / §5.1 T1–T2** — top-up inserts a `READY` row and advances `cursor_seq`; claim requires
  `now >= visible_at`. Both already do exactly what scheduling needs; T1's insert learns one column
  and T2 is untouched.
- **§5.2 invariants** — this issue adds **I12-a** (no message is delivered before its `deliver_at`,
  across restarts and clock steps) and **I12-b** (admitted-but-not-yet-due rows are bounded by
  config), and *amends* the reading of **I5** that #9 already made an admission invariant.
- **§4.2 schema** — one nullable `messages.deliver_at`, one partial index, one consumer column.
- **§9.2 / §9.4** — one new event name (`msg.schedule`), detail additions to three existing events,
  four metrics, and two corrections to existing gauges.
- **§14 / M9 exit criterion** — *"Each feature ships with a new invariant, events, metrics, docs — or
  it does not ship."* This issue is written to that bar. **D15** also says phase 2 is *strictly
  demand-ordered*: this is not started because it is next in the file, it is started because someone
  asked.

What it unlocks: the `sleep` inside a worker goes away, `nak --delay` gains a publish-side sibling,
and the retry/reminder workloads that today force people onto a scheduler *and* a queue collapse
onto one binary. Nothing else in the backlog is blocked by it (§Dependencies).

## Scope

**In scope**

- **Publish-side schedule intake**: `Messq-Deliver-At` (RFC3339 or `@<unix-ms>`), `Messq-Delay`
  (Go duration with a required unit), the `deliver_at` / `delay_ms` fields on the NDJSON batch
  publish, parsing, validation, clamping, and the new error codes.
- **Migration `000N_delayed_delivery.sql`**: `messages.deliver_at`, the partial schedule index, the
  covering-index widening of `deliveries_ready`, and `consumers.max_scheduled`.
- **Top-up admission (T1) carrying the schedule onto the delivery row** — `visible_at =
  COALESCE(m.deliver_at, 0)` — plus the pure `DecideAdmit` planner that owns the two-budget rule.
- **The second budget**: `max_scheduled` per consumer, enforcement at admission, overshoot semantics,
  and the `scheduled` / `scheduled_full` hold reasons.
- **Accounting corrections**: `backlog`, `oldest_pending_age_ms` and `blocked_by` in `consumer info`
  and in #21's collector must stop lying in the presence of scheduled rows.
- **Retention interaction**: publish-time rejection when the schedule outlives `max_age_ms`;
  `retention.expire` / `retention.blocked` detail so an operator can tell intentional scheduling from
  a stuck consumer.
- **Events**: one new vocabulary entry `msg.schedule` (DEBUG, sampleable, fold-rule required) and
  `detail` additions to `msg.publish`, `msg.dup` and `msg.deliver`; `docs/log-schema.md` 1.0 → 1.1.
- **Metrics**: `messq_scheduled`, `messq_scheduled_total`, `messq_schedule_lateness_seconds`,
  `messq_scheduled_expired_total` (names/labels/units specified here, registered by #21's registry).
- **API surface**: `deliver_at` / `scheduled` on peek, message list, fetch envelope and publish ack;
  `?scheduled=1&due_before=` on the message list; `scheduled` / `next_scheduled_at` on consumer info.
- **CLI**: `messq pub --at | --after [--jitter]`, `messq peek --scheduled`, `messq pending
  --scheduled`, a `SCHED` column in `messq lag`, the `trace` line, and `doctor` findings.
- **Invariants I12-a / I12-b**, `verify` checkers `SC1`–`SC3`, a new `rapid` action
  (`publish_scheduled`) and the matching field in the `internal/model` reference model.
- **Docs**: `SEMANTICS.md` (normative section), `PROTOCOL.md`, `OPERATIONS.md` (retention and
  stampede paragraphs), README feature line, and one line in `docs/non-goals.md` — **messq schedules
  one message at one instant; recurrence, cron and calendars are not messq's job.**

**Out of scope**

- **Cancelling or rescheduling an already-published message.** Messages are immutable (§4.2) and
  `messq stream purge --seq` exists. Not planned; it needs its own issue and its own invariant,
  because a mutable `deliver_at` breaks SC1's equality and the I10 fold.
- **A per-consumer "ignore schedules" flag.** Scheduling is message-level and universal here, per the
  D15 sketch. Not planned.
- **Ordered-mode interaction** — a scheduled message blocking its subject queue until due is
  **#38**'s head-of-line-blocking display problem; this issue states the rule and hands over the test.
- **Smoothing a due-burst on the server** — per-consumer rate limiting, **#39**. This issue ships the
  client-side `--jitter` and documents `max_ack_pending` as the real throttle.
- **`messq dlq redrive --delay`** (redriving on a schedule) — a natural extension of **#29**; the
  primitive lands here, the CLI flag does not.
- **Metric registration, help strings, alert rules, dashboard panels** — **#21** (names, labels and
  units are specified here). **Event fan-out, slog handlers, the sampling allow-list** — **#19** (one
  row added to its vocabulary table, one fold rule). **The retention sweep itself** — **#27** (two
  `detail` fields, one counter, one publish-time guard). **Trace rendering** — **#20** (§9 gives the
  target lines and the fixture). **Upgrade-fixture harness** — **#34**, plus one golden data dir
  produced here.

## Design proposal

> Proposed starting point, not a mandate. Four items are marked **decision** because they are
> expensive to reverse once messages carry schedules on disk. Two are spec refinements needing a
> matching `SEMANTICS.md` edit in the same PR.

### 1. Where the code lives

```
internal/queue/schedule.go        ParseSchedule, Schedule, ScheduleSource — pure
internal/queue/admit.go           Budgets, Admission, DecideAdmit — pure (extracted from #9's top-up)
internal/store/migrations/000N_delayed_delivery.sql
internal/store/publish.go         (#7) schedule validation + deliver_at column
internal/store/consumer.go        (#9) top-up admission, the two-budget count, stats corrections
internal/verify/schedule.go       checkers SC1–SC3
internal/model/                   (#13) reference model gains DeliverAt + publish_scheduled action
internal/cli/pub.go, peek.go      --at/--after/--jitter, --scheduled
```

`internal/queue` still knows nothing about `database/sql`. The admission rule is lifted out of #9's
top-up loop into a pure function precisely so #13 can drive it and so this issue's two-budget change
is a reviewable diff rather than an edit inside a SQL loop.

### 2. Semantics, stated before any code (**decision**)

1. **A schedule is a property of the message, not of a delivery.** One `deliver_at` per message,
   applying to every consumer, present and future. A consumer created after publish still sees the
   message at `deliver_at`.
2. **`deliver_at` is an absolute instant in broker time**, stored as Unix ms. `Messq-Delay` is
   resolved **once, at publish**, against the message's (monotone-clamped, #7) `published_at` — it
   is never re-evaluated.
3. **Scheduling delays *delivery*, not *storage* or *inspection*.** The message has a `seq` the
   instant it is published; `peek` shows it, `trace` shows it, retention counts it. Hiding it would
   make `seq` non-monotone in publish order and make the flagship forensic command lie.
4. **`deliver_at <= published_at` means "not scheduled"** — stored as `NULL`. A client computing
   `now + 5s` under clock skew must not get an error. The effective value is echoed in the publish
   response, so a clamp is never silent.
5. **`deliver_at` is immutable.** See Out of scope.
6. **Delivery is at-least-once and *at-or-after*, never before** (I12-a) — not a real-time guarantee.
   The message becomes *claimable* at `deliver_at`; when it is claimed depends on consumer capacity,
   like every other message. `messq_schedule_lateness_seconds` is the honest measurement and
   `SEMANTICS.md` says so in these words.

### 3. Wire surface

```console
$ curl -s --unix-socket /run/messq/messq.sock \
    -H 'Messq-Subject: orders.reminder' \
    -H 'Messq-Deliver-At: 2026-11-24T09:00:00Z' \
    --data-binary @reminder.json \
    http://localhost/v1/streams/orders/messages
{"stream":"orders","seq":10495,"id":"01J8ZQ4K2M9V0X7Y3B5N6C8D1E",
 "trace_id":"4bf92f35…","duplicate":false,
 "deliver_at":"2026-11-24T09:00:00.000Z","deliver_at_ms":1795777200000,"scheduled":true}
```

| Header | Grammar | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `Messq-Deliver-At` | RFC3339 with an offset (`…Z` / `…+02:00`), **or** `@<unix-millis>` | Sub-ms precision is truncated toward zero. A naive timestamp (no offset) is `bad_deliver_at` — guessing a timezone is how you ship a bug that fires at 03:00. |
| `Messq-Delay` | Go duration with a unit: `30s`, `90m`, `2h30m` | A bare integer is `bad_deliver_at` naming the missing unit. Negative → `bad_deliver_at`. |

Both present → `400 bad_request` (#7's rule for `?subject=` vs `Messq-Subject`, applied verbatim:
never guess which one the caller meant). Batch publish takes `"deliver_at"` (string, same grammar) or
`"delay_ms"` (integer) per NDJSON line, with the same mutual exclusion.

New error codes — additive to the closed enum, which is allowed post-1.0 and stated in
`PROTOCOL.md`:

| Code | Status | When |
|---|---|---|
| `bad_deliver_at` | 400 | unparseable, naive, negative, or both headers present |
| `deliver_at_too_far` | 400 | `deliver_at - now > --max-schedule-horizon` |
| `scheduled_beyond_retention` | 409 | the schedule outlives the stream's `max_age_ms` (§8) |

Read paths gain two fields wherever a message is rendered (peek, message list, fetch envelope, DLQ
show): `"deliver_at":"2026-11-24T09:00:00.000Z"` and `"scheduled":true` (`deliver_at` omitted and
`scheduled:false` when `deliver_at IS NULL`). The message list gains `?scheduled=1`, `?due_before=`
and `?due_after=`, served by the partial index in §4.

```go
package queue

type ScheduleSource uint8 // ScheduleNone | ScheduleAbsolute | ScheduleRelative

type Schedule struct {
    DeliverAt int64          // unix ms; 0 = immediate (after clamping, rule 4)
    Source    ScheduleSource
    Clamped   bool           // the caller asked for a past instant
}

// ParseSchedule is total and never panics; publishedAt is #7's clamped stream timestamp.
func ParseSchedule(deliverAt, delay string, publishedAtMS int64, horizon time.Duration) (Schedule, error)
```

### 4. Schema (**decision**)

```sql
-- 000N_delayed_delivery.sql
ALTER TABLE messages  ADD COLUMN deliver_at INTEGER;                        -- NULL = immediate
ALTER TABLE consumers ADD COLUMN max_scheduled INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1000;

CREATE INDEX messages_sched ON messages(stream, deliver_at) WHERE deliver_at IS NOT NULL;

-- widen the hot counting index so the new SUM below stays index-covered (§6)
DROP INDEX deliveries_ready;
CREATE INDEX deliveries_ready
    ON deliveries(stream, consumer, state, visible_at, seq, attempts);
```

Notes:

- `ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMN` is metadata-only in SQLite and legal on `STRICT` tables; the `NOT NULL`
  column carries a default, as SQLite requires. No table rewrite, so the migration is O(1) on a
  100 GiB database — worth asserting in #34's upgrade test rather than assuming.
- **`deliver_at` lives on `messages`, not only on the delivery row** (*decision*). It must survive
  `seek` (which drops delivery rows, T10), consumer creation after publish, and re-admission after
  #9's `max_ack_pending` shrink. Storing it only on the row would silently convert a scheduled
  message into an immediate one on any of those paths. It also makes SC1 an unconditional equality.
- **Rejected: keeping the schedule in the `hdr` JSON.** No index, a JSON parse per scanned row in the
  top-up hot path, and a user-writable column deciding delivery time.
- `messages_sched` is partial, so it costs nothing for streams that never schedule; it serves
  `peek --scheduled`, `?due_before=`, the per-stream gauge and the retention guard.
- Schema version bumps; older binaries refuse the data dir (#5), newer binaries migrate forward
  (#34). One golden data dir is committed at the old version.

### 5. The schedule lands on the delivery row at top-up — and it *must* (**decision**)

```sql
-- top-up scan (#9 §4), one column wider
SELECT seq, subject, deliver_at FROM messages
 WHERE stream = :s AND seq >= :cursor
 ORDER BY seq LIMIT :scan_limit;

-- admission (T1), one expression different
INSERT INTO deliveries (stream, consumer, seq, subject, state, attempts,
                        visible_at, generation, delivered_at, last_reason)
VALUES (:s, :c, :seq, :subject, 0, 0, :visible_at, :generation, NULL, NULL)
ON CONFLICT (stream, consumer, seq) DO NOTHING;   -- :visible_at = COALESCE(deliver_at, 0)
```

That is the entire storage change on the delivery side. The reason it has to be *eager* — the row is
created when the cursor reaches the message, however far in the future its schedule is — is worth
stating because the obvious alternative is a trap:

> **The cursor decides each message exactly once.** #9's top-up advances `cursor_seq` past every seq
> it has examined, matched or not, and an ack is a `DELETE` (D5). So there is no durable record of
> "seen, deliberately not admitted, come back later": re-scanning below the cursor cannot distinguish
> a scheduled message that was never admitted from one that was admitted, delivered and acked. A lazy
> scheme would therefore either **redeliver acked messages** or **skip scheduled ones forever**. The
> only other option — stopping the cursor at the scheduled message — head-of-line-blocks the consumer
> for the entire delay, which is catastrophic for a stream with mixed traffic.

Eager admission also makes the wake path free: the row is `READY` with a future `visible_at`, so
#11's pass B (wake parked consumers whose `visible_at` came due) and #14's `NextVisibleAt` sleep cap
already fire at exactly the right moment. **No new timer, no new goroutine, no new sweeper pass.**

The discriminator, everywhere, with no new column:

| Row shape | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `state=0 ∧ attempts=0 ∧ visible_at > now` | **scheduled** — admitted, never delivered, not yet due |
| `state=0 ∧ attempts>0 ∧ visible_at > now` | **backoff** — naked or timed out, waiting to retry |
| `state=0 ∧ visible_at <= now` | ready to claim |
| `state=1` | in flight |

`attempts=0 ∧ visible_at>0` is reachable *only* by scheduled admission: nak and timeout both require
`INFLIGHT` (so `attempts>=1`), and recovery reclaim (T9) only touches `INFLIGHT` rows. That
uniqueness is what makes SC1 checkable and is asserted by a test that enumerates every writer command
that sets `visible_at`.

### 6. Two budgets, so scheduling cannot starve live traffic (**decision**, spec refinement)

The failure mode a single budget produces: publish 10 000 messages delayed 24 h onto `jobs`, and a
consumer with `max_ack_pending = 1000` admits 1000 scheduled rows, hits its bound, and **delivers no
live work for 24 hours**. Unacceptable. So `max_ack_pending` counts work, and a second, independent
bound counts schedules:

```sql
-- #9 step 3, one SUM wider; index-covered by the widened deliveries_ready
SELECT COUNT(*)                                                      AS pending_total,
       IFNULL(SUM(state), 0)                                         AS inflight,
       IFNULL(SUM(state = 0 AND attempts = 0 AND visible_at > :now), 0) AS scheduled
  FROM deliveries WHERE stream = :s AND consumer = :c;
-- work budget:      pending_now = pending_total - scheduled   vs. max_ack_pending
-- schedule budget:  scheduled                                 vs. max_scheduled
```

```go
type Budgets struct {
    PendingNow, MaxAckPending int   // work in progress
    Scheduled,  MaxScheduled  int   // admitted, not yet due; 0 bound = unlimited
}

type StopReason string // "" | "ack_pending" | "scheduled_full"

type Admission struct {
    Admit     bool
    VisibleAt int64      // COALESCE(deliver_at, 0)
    Scheduled bool       // VisibleAt > now
    Stop      StopReason // non-empty ⇒ leave the cursor at this seq and end the scan
}

func DecideAdmit(deliverAt int64, b Budgets, nowMS int64) Admission
```

Rules:

1. A **due** message (no `deliver_at`, or `deliver_at <= now`) is admitted iff
   `PendingNow < MaxAckPending`; otherwise `Stop = "ack_pending"` (#9's existing behaviour, byte for
   byte).
2. A **scheduled** message is admitted iff `MaxScheduled == 0 || Scheduled < MaxScheduled`;
   otherwise `Stop = "scheduled_full"`.
3. The budgets are **independent**: a full work budget does not stop the scan at a scheduled message,
   and a full schedule budget does not stop it at a due one. Neither kind of traffic can starve the
   other; only a message of the *exhausted* kind halts the cursor.
4. `Stop` always leaves `cursor_seq` **at** the refused seq (#9's flow-control rule) — flow control
   delays, it never drops.

> **Spec refinement (`SEMANTICS.md` §S15, extending #9's wording).** #9 already restated I5 as an
> *admission* invariant — *no top-up inserts a row when `pending >= max_ack_pending`* — with
> `pending <= max_ack_pending` as an at-rest consequence "except during a deliberate shrink". This
> issue adds the second legitimate overshoot: when N scheduled rows come due simultaneously they
> become work without any write, so `pending_now` can exceed `max_ack_pending` until they drain.
> #8's I5 check reports it as an **advisory** with `reason="schedule_due"`, not a violation, exactly
> as it does for the shrink case.

**Cost, stated honestly.** The counting query walks the whole `(stream, consumer)` index prefix:
`O(pending + scheduled)` per fetch. At the default `max_scheduled = 1000` that is the same order #9
already pays. Raising it is a supported, measured trade — `BenchmarkFetchScheduled` records the cost
at 10³/10⁴/10⁵ rows and the number goes in `OPERATIONS.md` next to the flag. **Revisit trigger**
(D1's idiom): if a deployment needs ≫ 10⁴ scheduled rows per consumer at a high fetch rate, add a
static `deliveries.sched` column (set at admission, cleared at claim) as the *first* column of
`deliveries_ready`; the work budget then becomes a contiguous range count bounded by
`max_ack_pending` regardless of schedule volume. A migration and two query edits behind the store
package, invisible on the wire — and not worth paying today.

**Hold reasons and `blocked_by`** (extending #9/#14's closed sets):

- `scheduled` — pending > 0, nothing due, and every invisible row has `attempts = 0`. The fetch
  returns `retry_after_ms = next_visible_at - now`.
- `backoff` keeps its meaning: any invisible row with `attempts > 0` wins the ordering.
- `scheduled_full` — the admission cap was hit; the response names `scheduled` and `max_scheduled` so
  the operator sees the knob, and `flow.blocked` is emitted with `hold_reason="scheduled_full"`
  (existing event, existing repeat limiter).
- `blocked_by` order becomes: `paused` → `flow_control` → `scheduled_full` → `backoff` →
  `scheduled` → `catching_up` → `empty` → `none`.

### 7. Clock discipline and I12-a

Every comparison against `deliver_at` uses the writer's **batch timestamp**, which #6/#11 already
clamp monotone (`batchNow() = max(clock.Now(), lastNow)`). Consequences, which are I12-a's exact
wording:

> **I12-a.** For every message with `deliver_at = T`, no `msg.deliver` event exists with `ts < T`,
> where `ts` is the broker's clamped clock — across restarts, seeks, redrives and clock steps.

- **Backwards wall-clock step:** impossible to deliver early; the clamp means "now" never moves back,
  so a schedule that has not matured stays immature.
- **Forwards wall-clock step:** a jump of +1 h makes every schedule inside that hour due. That is
  *correct* — the broker's clock is the only clock it has — and identical to what #11 does to ack
  deadlines. It is made visible, not hidden: #11's tick-to-tick step detector (> 5 s) logs one WARN
  and stamps `detail.clock_step_ms` on the affected `msg.schedule`/`msg.deliver` rows, so `trace`
  explains the burst. #32 owns the injection test; this issue owns the assertion that no delivery is
  early *with respect to the clamped clock*, and `SEMANTICS.md` states the caveat plainly rather than
  implying NTP-proof scheduling.
- **Broker down across a deliver_at:** the row is durable and untouched (recovery reclaim only
  rewrites `INFLIGHT` rows), so the message is due the moment the daemon is back. The downtime shows
  up in `messq_schedule_lateness_seconds`, which is what that histogram is for.

### 8. Retention: the trap, and the two guards

`limits` retention deletes by `max_age_ms` from `published_at`, and a scheduled message is by
definition old. Two distinct cases, two distinct answers:

1. **No delivery row yet** (no consumer, or the cursor has not reached it): retention deletes it and
   the scheduled work silently never happens. **Guard: reject at publish** with
   `scheduled_beyond_retention` when `retention='limits' ∧ max_age_ms > 0 ∧ deliver_at -
   published_at >= max_age_ms`, in #7's teaching-error format:

   ```
   409 scheduled_beyond_retention
   stream "orders" keeps messages for 7d; this message is scheduled 30d out and would be
   deleted 23d before it could be delivered.
     fix   messq stream edit orders --max-age 40d
     or    publish to a stream with longer retention
   ```

   `max_msgs`/`max_bytes` pressure cannot be predicted at publish; that residue is covered by
   guard 2 and by `messq_scheduled_expired_total`, which is the number that makes the loss auditable
   instead of mysterious.
2. **A delivery row exists**: §4.5 already forbids deleting a message with an outstanding delivery
   row and emits `retention.blocked` — correct behaviour, wrong alert. A stream with a week of
   scheduled work would page an operator every sweep. **Guard: `retention.blocked` gains
   `detail.reason="scheduled"`** (#27 already carries a `reason` field), the shipped alert rule in
   `contrib/prometheus/alerts.yml` excludes it, and `messq_scheduled{stream,consumer}` is the gauge
   to alert on instead. `retention.expire` gains `detail.scheduled=N` counting scheduled messages it
   deleted before their time, feeding `messq_scheduled_expired_total`.

### 9. Events, and one new name

Per #19's rule 3, adding a `Kind` is allowed and must be a deliberate one-file diff. One is added:

| Event | Level | Smpl | Rpt | Fold | `detail` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `msg.schedule` | DEBUG | **yes** | no | **yes** | `deliver_at`, `delay_ms`, `consumer`, `seq`, `attempt:0` |

Emitted once per `(consumer, seq)` when a delivery row is admitted with `visible_at > now`. Justified
by the product thesis, not tidiness: "my job was supposed to run at 09:00 and did not" has four
distinct answers — *never admitted* (cursor behind, or `scheduled_full`), *admitted and waiting*,
*due but not claimed* (flow control), *delivered*. Without this row the first two are
indistinguishable in `trace`, which is exactly the class of question messq exists to answer. Volume
equals `msg.deliver` volume for scheduled traffic; it is DEBUG and sampleable, and per-message
sticky sampling (§9.3) keeps each story whole.

Detail additions to existing events (no new names): `msg.publish` gains `deliver_at`, `delay_ms`,
`source:"absolute"|"relative"`, `clamped`; `msg.dup` gains `deliver_at_differs`; `msg.deliver` gains
`scheduled_for` and `lateness_ms` on the first delivery of a scheduled message.

`docs/log-schema.md` goes to **1.1** — additive: new event names and new `detail` keys are
backwards-compatible, renames are not. #19's generated-doc diff and `TestVocabConsistency` enforce
it, and #13's fold model gains the `msg.schedule` rule (it creates a row state that no other event
explains, so I10 needs it).

### 10. Metrics (registered by #21; labels are `stream`/`consumer` only, §9.4)

| Metric | Type | Labels | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `messq_scheduled` | gauge | `stream`,`consumer` | admitted rows not yet due (collector, 5 s cache) |
| `messq_scheduled_total` | counter | `stream` | publishes carrying a schedule |
| `messq_schedule_lateness_seconds` | histogram | `stream`,`consumer` | first delivery − `deliver_at`. **The feature's SLI**; a negative observation is impossible and is a test assertion, not a clamp |
| `messq_scheduled_expired_total` | counter | `stream` | scheduled messages retention deleted before their time — configured data loss, made countable |

**Two corrections to existing series, which are the whole "don't page on intentional scheduling"
requirement** (spec refinement, `SEMANTICS.md` §3 definitions):

- `messq_backlog` / `consumer info.backlog` **exclude** not-yet-due rows, and `consumer info` reports
  `scheduled` and `next_scheduled_at` separately. A number that mixes "behind" with "parked on
  purpose" is useless for the thing it exists for.
- `messq_oldest_pending_age_seconds` — the SLI PLAN §9.4 tells operators to alert on — excludes
  not-yet-due rows and measures the rest from `COALESCE(m.deliver_at, m.published_at)`:

  ```sql
  IFNULL(MIN(CASE WHEN d.state = 1 OR d.visible_at <= :now
                  THEN COALESCE(m.deliver_at, m.published_at) END), 0) AS oldest_due_at
  ```

  Otherwise a message parked for 24 h reports 24 h of lag the instant it becomes due and pages
  everybody for working exactly as designed.

### 11. CLI

```console
$ messq pub orders orders.reminder -f reminder.json --after 24h
scheduled  seq=10495  01J8ZQ4K2M9V0X7Y3B5N6C8D1E  312 B  due 2026-11-24T09:14:02Z (in 24h0m)

  next  messq peek orders --scheduled
        messq trace 01J8ZQ4K2M9V0X7Y3B5N6C8D1E

$ messq peek orders --scheduled --due-before 2026-11-25T00:00:00Z
SEQ     DUE                       IN       SUBJECT           SIZE
10495   2026-11-24T09:14:02Z      23h45m   orders.reminder   312 B
10496   2026-11-24T09:14:02Z      23h45m   orders.reminder   298 B

$ messq lag
STREAM  CONSUMER  PENDING  INFLIGHT  SCHED  BACKLOG  OLDEST  BLOCKED BY
orders  worker          2         1   4998        0      1s  none
```

- `--at <RFC3339|@unix-ms>` and `--after <duration>` are mutually exclusive; both wrong-format cases
  fail **client-side** with exit `2` and a corrected example, before the round trip (#24's rule).
- `--jitter <duration>` spreads `--count N` publishes uniformly over `[at, at+jitter)`. It is
  computed in the CLI — **the server never invents a delivery time** — and exists because a 50 000
  message blast scheduled at exactly 09:00:00 is a self-inflicted thundering herd. `OPERATIONS.md`
  says so and names `max_ack_pending` as the real throttle.
- `messq pending` gains state `scheduled`, hidden by default (the command exists to find *stuck*
  work) and shown with `--scheduled` or `--all`.
- `messq consumer add/edit --max-scheduled N`.
- `doctor` findings: a stream whose scheduled horizon approaches `max_age_ms`; a consumer at
  ≥ 80 % of `max_scheduled`; `max_scheduled = 0` (unlimited) with > 10⁴ rows, naming the fetch cost;
  ≥ 10 000 messages due in the same second, naming `--jitter`.

### 13. Edge cases, decided

| Case | Handling |
|---|---|
| `deliver_at` in the past / equal to now | Clamped to "not scheduled": `deliver_at` stored `NULL`, `msg.publish detail.clamped=true`, response says `scheduled:false`. Never an error (rule 4). |
| Both `Messq-Deliver-At` and `Messq-Delay` | `400 bad_request` naming both headers. Never guess. |
| `Messq-Delay: 300` (no unit) | `400 bad_deliver_at`: *"durations need a unit: 300s, 300ms, 5m"*. |
| Naive RFC3339 (`2026-11-24T09:00:00`) | `400 bad_deliver_at`. A missing offset is a 03:00 pager. |
| Same `Messq-Msg-Id`, different `deliver_at` | Dedup wins (JetStream semantics, #7): the original is returned **with the original's `deliver_at`**, no reschedule; `msg.dup detail.deliver_at_differs=true` makes the "my idempotency key is not unique" bug visible. |
| Schedule outlives `max_age_ms` | `409 scheduled_beyond_retention` at publish (§8). |
| `max_msgs`/`max_bytes` evict a scheduled message anyway | Counted in `retention.expire detail.scheduled` and `messq_scheduled_expired_total`; never silent. |
| Retention blocked by a scheduled row | `retention.blocked detail.reason="scheduled"`, excluded from the shipped alert (§8). |
| Consumer created after publish, before `deliver_at` | Sees it normally at `deliver_at` — schedules are message-level. A consumer created *after* `deliver_at` with `start=first` gets it immediately; that is catch-up, not early delivery, and I12-a still holds. |
| `seek` (T10) drops the scheduled rows | Re-admitted at the next top-up with `visible_at` re-derived from `messages.deliver_at`. Self-healing — the reason §4 puts `deliver_at` on the message. Named test. |
| `max_ack_pending` lowered (#9's shrink) | The shrink drops tail rows matching `state=0 ∧ attempts=0`, which includes scheduled rows. Safe (the schedule is re-derived on re-admission) but wasteful, so the shrink prefers non-scheduled rows and only then drops scheduled ones; the cursor rewind is #9's, unchanged. |
| `max_scheduled` lowered below the current count | Accepted, no rows dropped, no schedule lost: the cap is an **admission** bound. Surplus drains as messages come due, reported as `{"overshoot": N}` exactly like #9's `max_ack_pending` case. |
| A scheduled message is naked after delivery | Ordinary backoff (`attempts>0`), never "scheduled" again. The discriminator stays clean; `visible_at` is rewritten by T4/T8 and SC1 stops applying to that row, which is why SC1 keys on `attempts=0`. |
| A scheduled message dead-letters | The DLQ copy has `deliver_at = NULL` (it is due *now*; re-delaying it in the DLQ would hide it) and carries the new provenance header `Messq-Origin-Deliver-At`. #12's header trimming rules apply unchanged. |
| `dlq redrive` of an origin-scheduled message | Republished as immediate. The schedule already elapsed; re-applying it would park a known-failed message for another day. `--delay` on redrive is #29's, out of scope. |
| `messq replay` of a not-yet-due message (#28) | Preserves `deliver_at` by default (a replay of a schedule is a schedule); `--now` strips it. One line in #28's flag table, one test here. |
| 100 000 messages due at the same instant | They become claimable together; delivery is paced by `max_ack_pending` and fetch batch size, and lateness is measured, not hidden. `--jitter` at publish and #39's rate limiting are the two real answers; `OPERATIONS.md` says which to use when. |
| `max_scheduled = 0` (unlimited) with millions of rows | Legal, warned, and the per-fetch counting cost is documented and benchmarked (§6). `doctor` names it. |
| Ordered-by-subject consumer (#38) | A scheduled message blocks its subject queue until due — head-of-line blocking *displayed, not hidden*, per §6 of PLAN. Rule stated here, test and rendering handed to #38. |
| Paused consumer | Schedules mature normally; nothing is claimed until resume. Identical to #11's paused-timeout decision. |
| SIGKILL between admission and commit | The transaction rolls back; the message keeps its `deliver_at` and is re-admitted at the next top-up. Nothing is lost and nothing is early. Fault point `store.topup.after_schedule_admit` reserved for #32. |
| Store latched read-only (fsyncgate) | No admission, no publish; schedules already committed are unaffected and mature after recovery. |
| A scheduled message whose origin is purged under the row | #11's `LEFT JOIN` path already retires such rows; nothing new. |

### 14. Invariants and `verify` checkers

| ID | Statement |
|---|---|
| **I12-a** | No message is delivered before its `deliver_at`, measured on the broker's clamped clock, across restarts, seeks, redrives and clock steps. |
| **I12-b** | For every consumer with `max_scheduled > 0`, no top-up admits a scheduled row when `scheduled >= max_scheduled` (an admission invariant, like I5). |

| Checker | Statement |
|---|---|
| `SC1` | Every delivery row with `attempts = 0 ∧ visible_at > 0` has `visible_at = messages.deliver_at` for its `(stream, seq)` (skipped when the message row is absent). Schedule fidelity, and the fence against any code path that writes `visible_at` without a reason. |
| `SC2` | *(deep)* No `msg.deliver` event has `ts < deliver_at` for its message — I12-a, checked over the events table by `messq verify --deep`. |
| `SC3` | Scheduled row count per consumer `<= max_scheduled` (advisory when the bound was lowered — the overshoot case in §13). |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `Messq-Deliver-At` accepts RFC3339-with-offset and `@<unix-ms>`; `Messq-Delay` accepts a
      unit-bearing Go duration; naive timestamps, bare integers, negative delays and both-headers-set
      each produce the documented 4xx code, and every one of those responses names the fix.
- [ ] A `deliver_at` at or before `published_at` stores `NULL`, reports `scheduled:false` and
      `clamped:true`, and is delivered immediately — never an error.
- [ ] `deliver_at - now > --max-schedule-horizon` → `400 deliver_at_too_far`; a schedule at or beyond
      the stream's `max_age_ms` → `409 scheduled_beyond_retention` naming `messq stream edit`.
- [ ] Migration `000N` adds `messages.deliver_at`, `consumers.max_scheduled`, `messages_sched`, and
      the widened `deliveries_ready`; an `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` golden pins the top-up scan, the
      two-budget count (index-covered, **no table lookups**) and `peek --scheduled` to their indexes.
- [ ] Top-up admits a scheduled message eagerly with `visible_at = deliver_at`, advances the cursor
      past it, and emits exactly one `msg.schedule` per `(consumer, seq)`.
- [ ] **I12-a**: a message scheduled at T is never claimed before T — asserted in `synctest` at the
      millisecond boundary (`T-1ms` claims nothing, `T` claims it), across a restart, across a
      `seek`, and across a +1 h forward clock step (which delivers early in *wall* terms and is
      logged with `clock_step_ms`, never silently).
- [ ] **Restart preserves schedules**: SIGKILL with 10 000 scheduled rows outstanding, restart, and
      every row still carries its exact `visible_at`; recovery reclaim touches none of them;
      `verify` SC1 clean.
- [ ] **10⁵ scheduled messages** with staggered due times are each delivered at or after their
      `deliver_at` and within one `--sweep-interval` of it for a consumer with spare capacity, in
      `synctest` (`max_scheduled = 0`); `messq_schedule_lateness_seconds` records no negative
      observation and a p99 below one tick.
- [ ] **Two budgets**: a consumer with `max_ack_pending = 1000` and 10 000 scheduled rows outstanding
      keeps delivering newly published immediate messages at full rate (the starvation regression
      test, and the reason this issue exists in this shape).
- [ ] Admission stops at `max_scheduled` with `Stop="scheduled_full"`, leaves `cursor_seq` **at** the
      refused seq, emits `flow.blocked` with `hold_reason="scheduled_full"`, and loses nothing when
      the cap is later raised.
- [ ] A simultaneous due-burst that pushes `pending_now` above `max_ack_pending` is reported by
      `verify` as an I5 **advisory** with `reason="schedule_due"`, not a violation.
- [ ] `backlog`, `messq_backlog`, `oldest_pending_age_ms` and `messq_oldest_pending_age_seconds`
      exclude not-yet-due rows and measure from `COALESCE(deliver_at, published_at)`; a consumer
      whose only work is a 24 h schedule reports `oldest_pending_age = 0` and `blocked_by=scheduled`,
      and reports the correct (small) age the moment it becomes due.
- [ ] `consumer info` reports `scheduled` and `next_scheduled_at`; `stream info` reports the stream's
      scheduled count; `messq lag` shows the `SCHED` column.
- [ ] `msg.schedule` exists in the vocabulary as DEBUG/sampleable/fold, has a fold rule in #13's
      model, appears in the generated `docs/log-schema.md` at version 1.1, and per-message sampling
      keeps `publish → schedule → deliver → ack` whole or drops it whole.
- [ ] `retention.blocked` from a scheduled row carries `reason="scheduled"` and is excluded by the
      shipped alert rule; `retention.expire` reports `scheduled=N` and increments
      `messq_scheduled_expired_total`.
- [ ] Dedup on `Messq-Msg-Id` with a different `deliver_at` returns the original schedule unchanged
      and flags `deliver_at_differs`.
- [ ] The DLQ copy of a scheduled message has `deliver_at = NULL` and carries
      `Messq-Origin-Deliver-At`; redrive republishes it as immediate; `replay` preserves the schedule
      and `replay --now` strips it.
- [ ] `messq pub --at/--after/--jitter`, `messq peek --scheduled --due-before`, `messq pending
      --scheduled`, `messq consumer edit --max-scheduled` behave as the §11 transcripts show, and the
      transcripts run as testscript goldens.
- [ ] `verify` SC1–SC3 are registered in #8's registry and run after every test in the packages
      touched here; `verify --deep` proves SC2 over a scenario containing every path that writes
      `visible_at`.
- [ ] `SEMANTICS.md` (delayed delivery, I12-a/b, the I5 refinement, the clock caveat),
      `PROTOCOL.md`, `OPERATIONS.md` (retention, stampede, the `max_scheduled` cost) and
      `docs/non-goals.md` ("no recurrence, no cron") land in the same PR; every doc command block
      executes in CI.
- [ ] Coverage floors hold (`internal/queue` ≥ 90 %, `internal/store` ≥ 85 %); no `time.Now()`
      outside the `Clock` seam; no `time.Sleep` in tests.
- [ ] `BenchmarkFetchScheduled/{1e3,1e4,1e5}` and a re-run `BenchmarkClaim`/`BenchmarkTopUp` (index
      widening cost) are recorded in the PR with hardware and durability mode stated.

  durations with and without units, negative, zero, naive, both-set, empty, whitespace, values past

  a full work budget never stops the scan at a scheduled message and vice versa.
